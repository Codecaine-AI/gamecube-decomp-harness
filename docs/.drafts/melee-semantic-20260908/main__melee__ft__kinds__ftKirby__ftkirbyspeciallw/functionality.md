## Kirby Stone down-special

This unit implements grounded and aerial Stone entry, transformation presentation, active-state maintenance, slope-sensitive movement, ending behavior, collision conversion, and presentation cleanup.

### Entry and transformation
Both entry routines clear command slots and transformation controls, load maximum/minimum Stone durations, assign two -1 sentinels, initialize terrain histories, clear HP/status, enter their named startup states, initialize callbacks/animation, and spawn effect 0x49B. Command 1 is accepted only while idle and not transformed: it initializes Stone HP, selects a form using modulo-five randomization, and begins forward sequencing. Command 2 starts reverse sequencing only while idle and transformed. All command values are consumed, including unknown values and commands whose guards fail. The different-form guarantee assumes the previous form is in 1..5. Model sequencing accesses indices 0..21 although the declared table has 23 entries. Rendered helper names are hypotheses supported by these operations, not independent proof.

### Animation, input, and recovery
Startup completion enters the corresponding named active state and freezes animation while preserving x221C_b4. Active animation callbacks set the active flag and service transformation helpers without local transitions. Active IASA exits on timeout or button mask 0x200 after the configured minimum interval; otherwise a positive countdown is decremented. Ground-origin exit calls ftCommon_8007D5D4, which explicitly makes the fighter airborne. Four startup/ending IASA callbacks are empty. Both ending animations service transformation, then clear HP/status on completion and select ordinary Fall or ftCo_80096900. That initializer has an x2224_b2 early diversion before FallSpecial initialization; jump consumption and conversion must retain their ground/air branches.

### Terrain and physics
Slope sampling stores the floor normal and facing-relative angle, probes nearby terrain, and cancels ground acceleration/velocity when normals oppose. Probe failure substitutes p->vec but still executes the sign tests; its value cannot be established from adjacent source declarations alone. History processing shifts four samples, normalizes their vector sum, averages angles, and derives damping through nested threshold/sign tests. All-shallow history leaves damping at 1.0; an inspected shallow sample beneath an above-threshold anchor produces 0.9, while opposing pairs can produce repeated 0.25 decrements clamped at zero.

Ground physics rotates the model by the mean angle, dead-zones/clamps a movement copy, chooses friction or slope acceleration, applies damping, bounds current and next-step speed, and projects velocity onto the floor. The wrapper's initial vertical clear is subsequently overwritten by this projection. Aerial startup and ending physics use animation-translation velocity replacement. Active aerial physics then overwrites vertical velocity with negative speciallw_gravity rather than integrating gravity.

### Collision and lifetime
Ground-origin collision transition branches reset histories, clear horizontal velocity, apply airborne conversion, preserve the status flag, and reinstall damage/death callbacks. Startup uses literal destination 0x18C; active uses named SpecialAirLw and freezes animation. Their alternate branches update slope sampling/history. Aerial collision transition branches apply grounded conversion, use literal destinations 0x189 and 0x18A, reinstall callbacks, emit landing sound/quake/helper feedback, and seed terrain histories. Alternate branches reset histories and tilt. Both ending collision callbacks refresh collision and reset histories/tilt unconditionally without local transitions. Reset sequences clear x88[0..7], not damping x88[8]. Collision-query GroundOrAir labels must not be equated directly with fighter ground_or_air.

Cleanup calls ftCommon_8007F5CC with 1, restores flags, clears HP/status and tilt, and destroys effects. Shared Kirby damage/death dispatchers call it outside this TU, so its lifetime is not Stone-exclusive. Hammer registration and the visibility dispatcher's canonical semantics remain verification followups. No compiled section-allocation or cast-layout claim is made.
