## Scope and identity
This TU comprises `ftCo_CaptureKirby.c` (129 lines) and its public header (17 lines). It implements the captured fighter's initial Kirby neutral-special capture state, a squared-distance helper used by Kirby's capture consumers, and an accessory callback for capture-relative placement and scaling. The canonical exported names are `ftCo_800BD19C`, `ftCo_800BD1DC`, `ftCo_800BD39C`, and the four `ftCo_CaptureKirby_{Anim,IASA,Phys,Coll}` callbacks. `ftCo_CaptureKirby_Enter` and `ftCo_CaptureKirby_Accessory1_Cb` are reasonable inferred names, not recovered canonical names. The header declares all seven functions and depends on fighter forward declarations and Dolphin vector types. ## Distance helper and consumers
`ftCo_800BD19C(gobj, pos)` reads `gobj->user_data` as Fighter and returns the sum of squared differences between `cur_pos` and all three components of `*pos`. It does not take a square root, mutate either input, validate pointers, or itself change state. Its second pointer is not const-qualified despite read-only use. Coincident finite positions yield zero; overflow and non-finite inputs are not sanitized. Evidence: code://c302741689bd67c361cd7faadb221df3193992c3/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftCommon/ftCo_CaptureKirby.c#L15-L20.
Kirby's `ftKb_SpecialNCapture_Anim` constructs the target from Kirby's position, a facing-scaled X inhaled offset, and a Y inhaled offset. A strict comparison against `SQ(specialn_inhale_velocity)` calls `ftCo_800BD620` on the captured fighter and selects numeric Kirby states 0x178 for air or 0x166 otherwise. Equality does not advance; unordered comparisons do not advance under ordinary IEEE comparison semantics. Negative finite velocity still produces a positive squared threshold. The active caller proves capture advancement but does not by itself prove that those numeric states are named Eat and EatAir. Another consumer, `ftKb_SpecialN_800F5EA8`, uses the same distance gate and selects `ftKb_SpecialN_800F63EC` or `ftKb_SpecialN_800F6388`. Evidence: code://c302741689bd67c361cd7faadb221df3193992c3/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftKirby/ftkirbyspecialn.c#L814-L833 and code://c302741689bd67c361cd7faadb221df3193992c3/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftKirby/ftkirbyspecialn.c#L314-L333.

## Accessory update, order, and boundaries
`ftCo_800BD39C` constructs a temporary vector from the stored XY residual with Z=0, invokes scaling first, then invokes residual reduction and position maintenance. Therefore scale reflects the pre-step residual, not the just-updated position. `inlineB0` computes `d=lbVector_Len(pos)` and, only when `d<R` for `R=x8.x`, computes `s=(1-k)+(d/R)*k`, where `k=x8.y`. It writes root-model scale X as `s*fp->mv.co.guard.x2C`, Y as `s*capturekirby.scale.y`, and Z as `s*capturekirby.scale.z`. The X operand is canonically the guard field: equivalence to saved `capturekirby.scale.x` is an unresolved object-layout claim, not proved here. At or outside the threshold there is no scale write or restoration. For finite nonnegative d, nonpositive R prevents the division branch; positive R gives factor 1-k at zero, approaching 1 at the threshold. Actual shrinking requires suitable k; no clamp guarantees a positive factor or restricts k to [0,1]. NaN comparison operands skip the branch under ordinary IEEE semantics; infinities and arithmetic overflow are not handled, and a non-finite k can produce non-finite scale. Exact target floating-point lowering is not established. Evidence: code://c302741689bd67c361cd7faadb221df3193992c3/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftCommon/ftCo_CaptureKirby.c#L75-L98 and code://c302741689bd67c361cd7faadb221df3193992c3/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftCommon/ftCo_CaptureKirby.c#L117-L128.
`inlineB1` independently limits each residual component only when its absolute value is strictly greater than its saved limit, selecting sign using `component < 0`. It subtracts those components from the persistent residual, then refreshes the partner capture point and writes XY as that point plus the new residual, while Z is copied directly from the point. With finite nonnegative limits, this is bounded, non-overshooting componentwise convergence; equality removes the residual in that call, zero limits stall nonzero residuals, and negative limits can reverse movement and even generate displacement from zero. NaNs and infinities have no validation path; comparisons or infinity subtraction can invalidate the ordinary convergence interpretation. Neither helper performs a motion-state transition. Evidence: code://c302741689bd67c361cd7faadb221df3193992c3/src/melee/ft/kinds/ftCommon/ftCo_CaptureKirby.c#L100-L128.
