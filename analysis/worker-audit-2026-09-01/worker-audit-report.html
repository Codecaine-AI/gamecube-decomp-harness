Cycle 02a80f9b · Run 4a45af8a · Epochs 1–13

Melee Worker Audit

What the 107 workers that reached a byte-exact match did differently from the 519 that stalled within half a percent of one — from tool logs, self-summaries, and deep transcript reads.

1,627 workers audited 215,634 tool calls parsed 44 exact/near-miss pairs deep-read 2026-09-01

TL;DRFive findings

1 · Nobody misreads the diff — stallers fail to act on it.

Across all 44 deep-read pairs, genuine diff misreads: zero. The dominant failure (36 of 43 real stalls) is post-diagnosis plateau churn: the worker correctly names the residual mismatch, then keeps re-rolling declaration/cast/permuter variants that reproduce the same diff instead of escalating to a structural change.

2 · Winners convert the diff into a predictive compiler hypothesis.

In 23 of 44 pairs, the exact worker mapped the differing instruction or register to a specific MWCC mechanism (a lifetime, a coalescing edge, an inline boundary, a section-layout rule) and then tested only source shapes predicted to move it. Exact workers were rated systematic in 20/44 deep reads vs 7/44 for their matched controls.

3 · The permuter is a stall correlate, not a closer.

Near-misses ran source_permuter_run far more (74.5% vs 57.0% of workers; ~2× the permuter-per-build ratio), with searches of 1,000–5,200 candidates confirming local maxima. In self-summaries, permuter use appears in 65.9% of near-miss accounts vs 34.6% of exact ones (−31 pp, the single largest technique gap). Permuters closed gaps only when pointed at a bounded, already-named mismatch.

4 · Exact workers finish faster by looking things up.

Median 65 tool calls / 25 min for exact vs 81 calls / 42 min for near-miss — failure is longer, not lazier. Exact workers were far likelier to use history and knowledge tools: past_prs_search 96% vs 76%, ledger_search 98% vs 83%, knowledge_graph_search 81% vs 63%. The strongest form was target-specific: finding the accepted PR, matched sibling, or packed type for this symbol and converting it into the next experiment (11/44 pairs).

5 · A visible slice of "failure" is system, not process.

Two stalls were hard write-set blocks (fix proven, file out of scope), 9 pairs hit tooling gaps (stale permuter setups, wibo 32-bit failures, missing FPR allocator visibility), and both the deep reads and the fleet-wide sweep surfaced score/label disagreements — workers recorded exact whose strict summaries read 99.9%, and no-progress rows whose summaries claim validated 100%. Prompt changes won't fix these; harness changes will.

Convert the residual into a predictive compiler hypothesis 23/44 pairs

Map the differing instruction, register, slot, or byte range to a specific compiler mechanism, then test only shapes predicted to move it.

"closing the loop between individual asm mismatches, MWCC allocator evidence, and narrowly targeted source-shape searches" — pair 06-02
Escalate from local syntax to authored structure 15/44

When declarations, casts, and expression forms plateau, change the lifetime topology: a semantic helper, an inline boundary, indexed access, an owner/subobject model.

"it eventually stopped treating a register-only diff as a collection of local allocation accidents and reconstructed the likely authored abstraction boundaries" — pair 08-06
Recognize when the mismatch class changes, and switch tools 15/44

Relocations, symbol ownership, byte layout, alignment, and .sdata2 order are a different problem than codegen. Winners moved to object/section evidence instead of continuing allocator edits.

Recover target-specific authored source before prolonged coercion 11/44

Not generic history search — finding the accepted PR, exact commit, matched sibling, or packed type for this symbol and turning it into the next experiment.

"searched the symbol ledger and past PRs at the outset, got the specific lead that local types and declaration order had matched this function before" — pair 03-02
Use bounded matrices and ablation only after naming the causal values 9/44

Custom variant matrices and one-at-a-time cue removal to test a defined hypothesis. Raw permuter volume never separated outcomes; a 10,000-candidate search changed nothing for one control.

Decompose mismatch classes and freeze solved regions 9/44

Lock one cluster, reclassify the residual, repair frame/register/scheduling/relocation fallout separately — instead of letting every edit reshuffle everything.

All 36 plateau-churn stalls were diagnosable from the worker's own diff output — the same residual kept coming back and the worker kept sampling the same family of edits. Fleet-wide, the self-summary sweep tells the same story: regalloc alone accounts for 936 of 1,627 stall self-reports, and permuter searches "of roughly 1,000–5,200 candidates often confirmed local maxima" while "semantic helper recovery, natural indexing, lifetime changes, or stack calibration were more likely to close the last gap."

Worth stating clearly: six stalls were honest dead ends — correctly diagnosed residuals (anonymous aggregate stack homes, unavailable FPR coloring, allocator preference) that survived systematic search. Not every 99.9% is a process failure.

07Validation plan

The honest next step is an A/B, since 25/44 pairs carry target-difficulty confounds. Concretely: apply R1–R5 (plus R12 as a harness path) to the worker prompt for half of the next epoch's claims, hold the other half on the current prompt, and compare exact-rate and median time-to-exact within baseline-score bands. The near-miss pool is large enough (519) that one epoch (~100–180 workers) gives a usable signal on the plateau-churn rules specifically.

Independent of the A/B, the §05 harness items — score reconciliation, stale-baseline validation, permuter/wibo health checks, and a write-set escalation path — are unambiguous and don't need an experiment.
